## F1 — Touch & acknowledgment

**Lens verdict.** AccessMap speaks three press dialects where the thesis demands one hand: (A) `PressableScale` — a 0.97 scale spring + haptic that measures 1–2px of edge-shift on a 48pt control and vanishes entirely under Reduce Motion; (B) hand-rolled static `({pressed})` dims — the honest pattern (drawer rows, Tasks card, header circles, Profile, onboarding) that survives RM by construction; and (C) dead-to-the-hand raw Pressables — which happen to include the app's single most important surface, the Report sheet, plus the entire filtering language, the tab bar, and most modal closes. The *after*-channels are genuinely strong — selected fills, the polite count pill, cycle-tool announcements, confirm-gated Reject — but the *during*-channel R4 lives in ("with a tremor I frequently don't know if my touch landed") is absent on most of the app and merely rumored on Dialect A. One material evidence correction this lens owes the bank: the `tasks-card` and `tasks-verify` press captures held the wrong elements (`getByText` first-match landed on the dead Tasks category chip and the static "**Verify** and resolve reports" subtitle respectively), so R4's "even the big blue Verify shows nothing" is unproven against the real controls — code says the card dims statically and Verify scales; what those captures *accidentally* prove is that the Tasks filter chips are dead. Haptics exist as a well-built three-word vocabulary but are deployed semantically scrambled at the trust engine; `Button.tsx` remains at 0 call sites (census only — Fork 9, not relitigated). The one-hand *reach* half is a split verdict: everything shipped since Round 1 (zoom, List, Report FAB, pinned sheet footers) honors the bottom zone; the seven-tool rail stays another country at the top.
