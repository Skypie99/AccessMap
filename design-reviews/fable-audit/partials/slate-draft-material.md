## material-1 · Adopt the ratified `textOnColor` ink at the six un-forked severity-digit sites (CRITICAL)

**Resolves:** L2-1 / L6-08 / L6-10 (canonical C; + parked item ①; arbiter §D-1/§D-2)
**Effort:** S · **Tier:** must-ship (this is the audit's #3 CRITICAL and a live WCAG AA breach) · **Signature:** no (it's a mechanical law-extension — but it is the single most important material fix on the slate)

**What / why.** The system already retired white-on-severity-fill once (`92a2be6`) and ratified the fix as a token: `severity[n].textOnColor` at `theme.ts:543-547` puts ink `#0F1B2D` on fills 1–4 and keeps white only on sev-5 red (with the AA rationale written into the comment at :538-542). SeverityBadge, the Map sev pills (`MapScreen.tsx:1690-1700`), and the action-bar quick chip already obey it. But the white digit *survives in six components / seven render sites* that never adopted the fork — and the arbiter measured them head-on: white on the fills reads **1.57 / 2.15 / 2.78 / 3.61** (sev1–4) against the 4.5 floor, failing at every 4.5-floor site and even at the lenient 3.0 large-text floor for sev1–3. Three are guest-reachable and load-bearing: `NearbyFlagsModal.tsx:140-145` (the disc list that *auto-opens for every web visitor* and is the designed SR alternative to the map), `LegendModal.tsx:66-70` (the severity decoder ring itself), and `ReportFlagModal.tsx:607/637/1081-1084` (the *selected* severity button in the core CONTRIBUTE flow — a white "3" + white check on orange). Three are auth-gated: `ActivityFeedModal.tsx:156-162`, `RecentlyViewedRow.tsx:139/202-204` (= arbiter D-1, parked ①), `FlagDetailModal.tsx:1066/1076` (edit radio) plus the site-6b view-mode "Severity {n}" chip at `:834/:1595` the skeptic surfaced. Severity is THE trust datum of a barrier map; a sev-1 disc currently reads as a blank yellow dot on the one list every web guest sees, and the number a user just picked on the Report form is at its least legible the moment it's chosen — a direct contradiction of DESIGN.md's "born accessible… color is never the only signal." The fix is purely mechanical: swap the hardcoded `textOnBrand`/`sevTextActive` white at all seven sites for `severity[s].textOnColor`, and flip the paired white Check glyph in ReportFlagModal to the same ink. No new token, no floor tuning, no geometry change.

**Verification (named):** re-run `tools/audit-stacks.json` through `contrast-check.mjs` (the arbiter) with a stack per site (disc/selected-chip/edit-radio over each severity fill) and require exit 0; the transferable arbiter pairs already prove ink `#0F1B2D` = 8.05 / 6.21 / 4.79 on sev2–4, so the swap is provably AA. **This EXTENDS the law — it does not touch a floor** (GLASS §7.1 honored; the eye never decides). **Preserves PROTECT-4** (it makes the six holdouts finally obey the fork the crown-jewel already ratifies) and the severity-grammar signature (merged-list #4). A tempering note for the writer: every site already keeps a passing text twin nearby (Nearby's meta line, Legend's adjacent labels, Report's live label line), and all discs are a11y-hidden with the number in the SR label — so information is never *lost*; the primary visual mark is what fails, which is exactly why the mechanical adoption is safe and complete.

## material-2 · One editorial header family across every tab (SIGNATURE)

**Resolves:** L2-2 / L8-6 (canonical H) · absorbs the "double title on signed-in Profile" facet · touches L2-15 (close-affordance grammar) as a sibling normalization
**Effort:** M · **Tier:** should-ship (the audit's named strongest seam) · **Signature:** YES

**What / why.** This is the seam a user crosses on *every* tab switch and the strongest single "two kits stitched together" signal in the whole app (R5 friction #2; skeptic CONFIRMED HIGH, no ratification found — it's an incomplete Phase-7b migration, not a decision). AccessMap ships two navigation architectures at once: Home/Tasks wear the **editorial** header (`headerShown:false` + in-body `ScreenHeader`: uppercase eyebrow + display-40 title + 44px WHITE-CIRCLE actions + icon-only Feedback), while Profile/FullMap/Settings wear the **shared nav** header (centered bold-16 title + 36px rounded-square hamburger + a "Feedback" TEXT pill). The same drawer trigger is drawn two shapes, two sizes, two finishes; the same Feedback action is an icon on two tabs and a labeled pill on the rest; and on **signed-in Profile the two families literally stack** — the nav bar's centered "Profile" sits directly above the in-body "PROFILE" eyebrow, a redundant double title on a primary auth surface. The fix direction is already written into the system's own words: the `ScreenHeader` docstring declares the intent ("Profile / Leaderboard / future screens consume it for one consistent type rhythm", `ScreenHeader.tsx:8-10`), and PROTECT-10 nominates its deterministic auto-fit mechanism as the thing to *extend the family with, never replace*. The signature move: roll the editorial `ScreenHeader` onto Profile, FullMap, and Settings; set `headerShown:false` on those routes so the redundant nav bar disappears (killing the double title outright); unify the drawer trigger to ONE shape/size/finish and the Feedback affordance to ONE treatment (icon-in-header) everywhere; and while the family is being unified, converge the modal close-affordance grammar (X-in-circle-wash vs bare-X vs bordered "Close" pill — L2-15) on a single close idiom. The payoff is that the app finally reads as ONE editorial product at the top of every screen — the ownable, memorable, in-ethos identity Sky actually chose, executed to completion instead of half-migrated.

**Why signature-worthy & access-first.** It's memorable (the editorial eyebrow + display type is a real, distinctive house voice), ownable (it's *AccessMap's* chosen identity, not a generic pattern), and — critically for this audit — it carries **zero AA cost** (both header families' inks already pass the arbiter, A.1/A.3) while *improving* structure for SR users: removing the double header removes a redundant, confusing title from the signed-in Profile traversal. A signature that regressed access would be a contradiction here; this one advances it. **Preserves PROTECT-10** (it extends the ScreenHeader family exactly as the crown-jewel prescribes, using its auto-fit backstop rather than forking a new header). **Device caveat for the writer:** the double-header is `[code-inferred, auth-gated]` (PROBE-1 — needs an authed session or a composited lab mockup to *see* in pixels); the family split itself is fully capture-proven (`base/home__light__390` vs `base/settings/map/profile-signedout` — identical in both themes). Annotated capture exists: `assets/annotated/L2-2__two-header-families.png`.

## material-3 · Claim the flagship Map — theme the tiles, tame the third-party chrome (SIGNATURE, web-scoped)

**Resolves:** L2-3 / L8-5 (canonical I) · absorbs the dark-void-in-light-Home slice (L1-6) and the attribution slice (L5-16) · pairs with L2-4 for the Home peek
**Effort:** M · **Tier:** should-ship (first-contact surface for the entire guest cohort) · **Signature:** YES (co-signature with material-2)

**What / why.** The namesake screen is the one place the app "feels embedded, not built" (R5), and it's the *first* surface every web guest touches. Three defects, all web-scoped but all real-and-shipped (native renders none of this by construction): (1) the CARTO basemap is `dark_all` **unconditionally** (`PlatformMap.web.tsx:531`, a single const with no theme branch), so in light mode the map — and Home's dark map-peek — render near-black, the "failed render / black hole" three personas hit (R6's session-ending #1, R1, R3); (2) the raw Leaflet zoom `+/−` white rectangles sit untinted and *half-buried* under the app's own "N flags nearby" status pill (this is also the visual face of the L5-01 zoom lockout — occlusion of the only non-gesture zoom path); (3) the full-width Leaflet/OSM/CARTO attribution strip (Ukraine-flag glyph + blue web links) crosses the map bottom untouched. The signature move is to *own* the map surface the way the rest of the app is owned: branch the tile URL on `color.scheme` so light mode gets a light-family CARTO basemap (closing the black-hole first impression in both FullMap and the Home peek), style `.leaflet-control-zoom` into the Deep Field chrome grammar and reposition it clear of the status pill's safe area (removing the occlusion), and style/condense the attribution strip to the app's hairline voice — **kept, not removed** (OSM/CARTO attribution is legally required; this is a restyle, never a deletion). The result: the flagship stops looking like a third-party widget dropped into a premium shell and starts reading as a screen the same hands built — the strongest "one product" win available on the FIND half, mirroring material-2's win on the chrome half.

**Why signature-worthy & access-first.** Memorable and ownable (a themed, house-styled map is the visual heart of a *map* product), and access-positive: theming the tiles is what makes the light-mode map legible at all, and un-occluding the zoom control is the visible half of restoring the only non-gesture zoom path for dexterity users. **Preserves PROTECT-9** (the pinned-always-light *overlay* strategy — legend/locating-banner literals — is AA-by-construction over any tile and stays exactly as-is; this candidate themes the *basemap and third-party chrome*, never the app's own always-light overlays). **Honesty rails:** `[web-approximated / web-only]` throughout — the iOS build uses Apple tiles that follow the OS and is unaffected; the light-tile visual is NEEDS-SKY-DEVICE for the native side. **FORKS-TO-SKY:** the *zoom-lockout mechanism itself* (taps dying on the un-guarded `topRow` wrapper, and the iOS no-zoom-out gap) is canonical L5-01 and belongs to the interaction cluster — material-3 scopes only the *material/occlusion half* (theme + restyle + reposition the chrome); the pointer-dead `topRow` fix is L5-01's to make. Annotated capture: the L8-5 anchor under `assets/annotated/`.

## material-4 · Give the severity ramp one grammar: number + word everywhere (M)

**Resolves:** L2-7 · the "raw status enum leaks on Home" copy observation (L2 copy-index) · reinforces material-1
**Effort:** M · **Tier:** should-ship · **Signature:** no (but it directly serves THE signature — the severity grammar, merged-list #4)

**What / why.** Severity is the product's core vocabulary, yet it renders in **four visual grammars** and Home breaks the system's own written law. theme.ts:526 says "Each color is ALWAYS paired with a number + a word" and DESIGN.md §1 repeats it — but only **Tasks** (the SeverityBadge chip "1 · Minor") actually delivers both. Home Recent rows show a word without a number (and no number even in the SR label, `HomeScreen.tsx:322-334`); the Nearby/Activity/RecentlyViewed discs show a number without an adjacent word; Legend/Report show numbered circles with word labels beside them. R6's confusion arc is the lived cost ("severity took much longer — Home speaks words, the list speaks numbers… it only clicked on the report form's inline label"). One grammar already exists, is arbiter-proven, and is the strongest of the four: the SeverityBadge chip. The move is to converge every severity surface on **number + word** — bring the SeverityBadge chip (or its exact "N · Word" pattern) to Home's Recent rows and put the word inline on the disc surfaces (Nearby/Activity/RecentlyViewed) so the color, the number, and the word always travel together. While here, fix the paired copy leak the same lens flagged: Home rows print the raw lowercase DB enum `item.f.status` ("Minor · open") while every other surface uses `STATUS_LABELS` ("Open") — route Home through `STATUS_LABELS` so one surface stops speaking database while the rest speak product (R6 read "open" as business hours). Net effect: the safety vocabulary reads identically on every screen a user crosses, which is what a civic safety product's core datum demands.

**Verification & rails.** No contrast change of its own — but because it MUST land on top of material-1's ink fix (the discs that gain a word must already carry the ratified `textOnColor` digit), sequence material-4 after material-1, and re-confirm any newly-composed severity chip through the arbiter if it introduces a new fill/ink pair. **Preserves PROTECT-4 and the severity-grammar signature (merged-list #4) — it EXTENDS the "never speak severity in one channel" law** to the surfaces that currently speak it in one channel. Tempering for the writer: this is a cohesion + own-law fix with no standalone AA failure (the dots are already supplemented by words *somewhere* on-screen); its value is coherence and the elimination of R6's decode tax.

## material-5 · Unify the modal layer on one material (M)

**Resolves:** L2-5 (two-material modal layer, incl. the undisclosed half — Feedback's own siblings + the Map modals)
**Effort:** M · **Tier:** nice-to-have → should-ship (the Report sheet is on the un-tiered side) · **Signature:** no

**What / why.** The overlay tier is where cohesion is *cheapest to read* (same geometry, same scrim) — so a material split is naked there. Right now About and Feedback went bulk-glass (`AboutScreen.tsx:51`, `FeedbackModal.tsx:182`) while their **own SharedModalsHost siblings** — Help, Changelog, MyFeedback — stayed flat opaque pre-glass cards (0 `GlassSurface` refs each), and so did every Map sheet: NearbyFlags, Legend, ReportFlag, AddressSearch, plus the inline SavedPlaces/Preset prompts. A user can open Feedback (frosted bulk glass) and Help (flat opaque card) from the *same header button on the same screen* and get two different products. W2 disclosed the Profile children + SignIn as a "next mini-wave," but Help/Changelog/MyFeedback and the six Map modals were never listed or de-scoped anywhere in the repo — and the **Report sheet, the app's single most important surface, is on the un-tiered opaque side.** The move: extend the bulk-glass material to the sibling host modals (Help/Changelog/MyFeedback) so a host family reads as one material, and bring the Map sheets (at minimum Legend and Report, the guest-critical ones) onto the same tier — threading `GlassSurface` per the existing bulk recipe, never forking it. This closes the loudest remaining "two kits" seam on the cheapest-to-read layer and finally dresses the Report sheet in the system's material.

**Rails.** THREAD `GlassSurface.tsx` (DO-NOT-EDIT) with the ratified bulk recipe (floor 0.85 + i=24 — an allowed intensity); do NOT introduce any new blur intensity; every newly-glassed sheet's inks must be arbiter-checked against the bulk worst-case stack before it's called done (re-run `contrast-check.mjs`, exit 0). **Watch the L2-6 interaction:** raising the Report/Legend sheets onto light bulk glass inherits the ghosting *perception* question (material-8) — so if material-8's device call lands on "scrim the backdrop," apply it here too. **Preserves PROTECT-3** (the C-lite/engineered-twin parity and the budget mechanism — any new bulk sheet must keep its C-lite fallback path). Sequence after material-8's device decision where the two overlap.

## material-7 · The heat-legend swatch hairline (S)

**Resolves:** L2-8 (heat-legend swatches invisible where the map is hottest; concur arbiter D-3)
**Effort:** S · **Tier:** quick win · **Signature:** no

**What / why.** The heat legend floats on *every heatmap view*, and its 10×10 borderless color swatches (`HeatmapLegend.tsx:45/:87-91`) dissolve exactly where the layer peaks: the arbiter measured the heat1–4 swatch boundaries at **1.01 / 1.48 / 1.84 / 2.47** against the 3.0 floor (heat5 barely clears at 3.17) — the 1.01 worst case is the legend's low swatch floating over a sev-5 red heat cell, i.e. the color key vanishes precisely where the map is most dangerous. The Map report disclosed *skipping* this as a 1.4.11 color-sample exemption (a color sample's color is essential, so you can't just recolor it) — a defensible read for the fill itself. But the system already ratified the exact remedy for this problem class: the **1px `#0F1B2D` hairline union** it puts on cluster rings and heat badges. The heat-legend swatch is the one color-bearing map element that never got that hairline. The move: add the same ratified 1px `#0F1B2D` boundary to each legend swatch — the fill color (the essential sample) is untouched, but the swatch gains a definition edge over any heat cell behind it, matching the system's own internal precedent.

**Verification & rails.** Re-run `contrast-check.mjs` on the legend swatch-boundary stack (swatch edge vs the five heat bases + red cell) and require the hairline to clear 3.0 — the same check the cluster-ring/heat-badge union already passes. This is an **internal-consistency fix that adopts an existing ratified pattern**, not an eye-tuned floor (GLASS §7.1 honored). **Preserves PROTECT-9** (the pinned-always-light overlay strategy — the legend stays always-light AA-by-construction; this only adds the boundary the rest of the map's color-bearing elements already have). Tempering: each swatch already sits beside its "N Label" text and the SR label names the colors, so disclosure survives — it's the *color-key function* that degrades, which the hairline restores.

## material-8 · The light bulk-sheet ghosting decision (device-gated) (S–M)

**Resolves:** L2-6 (light bulk sheet reads as bleed-through over busy backdrops)
**Effort:** S–M · **Tier:** nice-to-have (device-gated) · **Signature:** no

**What / why.** This is a *perception* defect, not a contrast defect — every ink on the worst-case bulk stack already passes the arbiter (W1 §4). But the blinded senior reviewer read the light bulk sheet as a **rendering bug**: over a busy backdrop, high-contrast rectangles (Home's dark map-peek slab, the "5 barriers" headline, the red badge) survive the i=24 blur as recognizable gray shapes ghosting *through* the body text of About and Feedback (`base/about__light__390__at-rest.png`, `base/feedback-modal__light__390__at-rest.png`; R5: "reads as a rendering bug"). The two glass sheets are the material's ambassador moments — if the frost reads as a bug to a senior eye, it reads as one to users, and trust-through-polish is the mission. Two device-gated fix candidates: (a) raise the light bulk floor one step (e.g. 0.85 → a higher opacity) so high-contrast backdrop shapes stop resolving, or (b) drop a scrim under the sheet so the backdrop is knocked back before the blur ever sees it. This candidate exists mainly to *carry the decision into Part 3 with its rails attached*, not to prescribe a value.

**Hard rails (load-bearing here).** `[web-approximated]` — Chromium `backdrop-filter` is only a proxy; native gaussian blur at i=24 may diffuse edges differently, so the *whole* decision is **NEEDS-SKY-DEVICE** before any change (dark mode already hides this — dark floor over dark content). Any floor change is a change to a GLASS-governed material and **MUST re-run `contrast-check.mjs`** (GLASS §7.1 — the arbiter re-decides every ink on the new floor; the eye never sets it). Option (b) scrim is the lower-risk path (adds a layer rather than moving a ratified floor). **Preserves the arbitration crown jewel (merged-list #5)** — no eye-tuned floor ships; whatever value Sky's device eye picks is then *proven* by the arbiter, not asserted. Overlaps material-5 (if Report/Legend join the bulk tier, apply the same backdrop treatment there).
