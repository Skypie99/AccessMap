## Lens & method

I scored the ENTIRE surviving slate together so the rankings are relative. My charge weights **GOAL 1 (impact)** above all: can a person *with* a disability actually FIND barriers, understand+trust what they see, and CONTRIBUTE — via VoiceOver/TalkBack, x2 Dynamic Type, one hand, limited dexterity, or low vision? `cohesion` and `ethos` are recorded honestly but do not override impact in my role.

**Persona key** (from `01_baseline-reads.md`): R1 wheelchair/route-planner (risk-reading), R2 blind/VoiceOver, R3 low-vision/large-type/zoom, R4 one-handed/limited-dexterity, R5 senior-craft + the vestibular/reduce-motion proxy the slate maps the RM cohort onto, R6 first-timer under cognitive load.

**Spread discipline.** I did not cluster at 4–5. The five proposals that take a core disabled-user job from *impossible* to *possible* (or end a mission-betrayal outright) earn impact 5. Narrow-but-real floor fixes sit at 4. Single-cohort, twin-mitigated, device-gated, or copy-only-trust items sit at 3. The softest peripheral-trust repair sits at 2. Cohesion and ethos carry their own independent distribution.

## The one thing I want the report writer to hear

The audit's own finding — echoed by all six blinded readers — is that AccessMap is *two products*: a genuinely humane FIND/severity core, and a set of surfaces that silently shear that care off exactly where a disabled user needs it (the web SR engine, the dead Report pill, the lying arrival pill, the locked zoom). From my chair the slate is correctly ordered: **the CRITICAL-flow unblockers (S5, S4, S6, S9) and the two signature moves that make the safety datum honest and inspectable (S1, S3) are the ballgame.** Everything else is worthy, but if only six ship, ship those six — they are the difference between "an app about accessibility" and "an accessible app."

## Rationale for the sharpest calls

- **Why S9 = impact 5 despite "no visible change."** It is the only proposal that re-mounts four *entire* accessibility subsystems (selection state, announcements, decorative-hiding, modal labels) on the sole surface a guest has. R2 literally cannot confirm a category/filter selection on web today; the Tasks screen *opens on "image."* This is a mission breach, not polish. It sits with the flow-unblockers.
- **Why S12/S14/S17/S7 land at impact 3, not 4.** Each is a genuine floor or safety fix, but each is bounded: S12 to the motion-sensitive cohort on one camera move; S14 is twin-mitigated (the Nearby list already speaks severity) and native-only; S17 removes one disorienting failure class on one surface; S7's *access* leg (R3 light-mode legibility) is real but the bulk of its value is cohesion/trust. Honest spread requires not inflating these to match the flow-unblockers.
- **Why S20 = impact 2.** Correct FAQ/changelog/About copy matters for trust, and a wrong "Map tab / ＋ Report" instruction genuinely misdirects R6 and R2 — but it is the most peripheral surface on the slate (consulted *after* trouble), and every other proposal touches a primary job path. It is the floor of my impact distribution by design.
- **Impact 5 cluster, stated plainly:** S1, S3, S4, S5, S6, S9. Four are CRITICAL-flow (S4/S5/S6/S9) and two are the signature moves that make the safety datum honest and inspectable (S1/S3). If Sky ships a minimal set, this is it.

## personasServed methodology

I list a persona only where the proposal changes *their* ability to do a core job or ends a betrayal the baseline read named for them — not every persona who is incidentally present. Where the slate's own per-persona section (field 3) names a beneficiary, I honored it; where I judged a named persona to be only incidentally touched, I dropped them to keep the mapping load-bearing. R5 appears only on the two reduce-motion items (S12) where the slate explicitly maps the vestibular cohort onto R5.
